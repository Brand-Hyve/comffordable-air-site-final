## Key takeaways

- Most Florida homes need professional AC service **twice a year** — once before cooling season and once before heating season.
- Skipping maintenance is the single most common cause of avoidable mid-summer breakdowns.
- A tune-up typically pays for itself in energy savings and deferred repairs within one season.

## How often should you service your AC in Florida?

Twice a year. Florida's cooling season runs far longer than the national average, so a system here accumulates roughly double the runtime hours of an identical unit in a temperate climate. Two visits — spring and fall — keep refrigerant charge, airflow, and electrical connections inside spec before the load peaks.

## What does a maintenance visit actually include?

A proper visit is a multi-point inspection, not a filter swap. A technician checks refrigerant pressures, tests capacitors and contactors under load, clears the condensate drain, measures temperature split across the coil, and calibrates the thermostat. Anything drifting out of range gets corrected before it turns into a failure.

## What happens if you skip it?

Three things compound. Airflow drops as the coil and blower load up with debris, which raises energy use. Electrical components run hotter and fail sooner. And small refrigerant losses go unnoticed until the system can no longer keep up on the hottest day of the year — which is exactly when everyone else is calling too.

<dfn>Temperature split</dfn> is the difference between the air entering and leaving your system. A healthy split is typically 16–22°F; anything outside that range points to an airflow or refrigerant problem.

## Is a maintenance plan worth it?

For most homeowners, yes. A plan bundles both annual visits, adds priority scheduling during peak season, and usually includes a discount on any repair. The math works out in your favor the first time you need a service call in July.
